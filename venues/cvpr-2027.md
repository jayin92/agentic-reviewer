## What reviewers are asked to evaluate
- Each accepted paper "should be technically sound and make a contribution to the field." Look for "what is good or stimulating in the paper, and what knowledge advance it has made."
- "Embrace novel, brave concepts, even if they have not been tested on many datasets." Weigh "both the novelty and potential impact of the work alongside the reported performance."
- Write the main critique as a list of strengths and weaknesses, and explain each argument. "Bullet lists with one short phrase per bullet are NOT a detailed review." The detailed review matters more than the score.
- Be specific about novelty. Any "has been done before" claim MUST cite specific references and explain how closely they are related. For a positive review, summarize the most interesting novel aspects in the Strengths section.
- Be specific about writing problems: name the unclear section and suggest how to clarify it. "Well known" or "common practice in industry" claims need specific citations (publications, books or public disclosures).
- Give constructive feedback: new technical tools, datasets, application areas, or ways to generalize the idea. If the paper is out of scope, explain why and suggest better venues, but keep an open mind unless the mismatch is extreme.
- Check:
  - reproducibility (checking code is optional)
  - dataset-contribution claims (the dataset must be public by camera-ready)
  - attribution of data assets (comment in the corresponding review-form field)
  - personal or human-subject data (IRB or ethics statement; report problems to the AC/PCs, do not handle them yourself)
  - discussion of negative societal impact and of limitations (weigh both POSITIVELY)
- Responsible Reviewing Policy: reviews must "demonstrate meaningful engagement with the submission" and reference specific technical content from the paper. They must not be generic, and must not contain factual errors that show superficial reading.
- After the rebuttal (mandatory), use the final review form to:
  - acknowledge the rebuttal
  - reaffirm or update the scores
  - state briefly which decision-critical concerns, if any, remain unresolved
- AC acceptance criteria (context): papers are judged by significance (Award / Oral / Poster / Reject criteria below), not by a score curve.

## What reviewers are told NOT to do
- **LLM use:** do not use any LLM or chatbot in any part of the reviewing process except the official LLM assistant inside OpenReview. This covers LLMs run locally, via an API, or as a web service. Do not share the paper, supplementary material, review, rebuttal or discussion with any other LLM. Uploading a submission to an external LLM breaches IEEE confidentiality and counts as reviewer misconduct, which can lead to desk rejection of the reviewer's own papers. Reviews "generated by an LLM rather than by the reviewer's own assessment" are highly irresponsible. The official assistant does not write reviews or propose scores, and reviewers must enter scores and the recommendation themselves. (Note for this pipeline: an external, LLM-based review tool falls under this prohibition if the user is acting as an assigned CVPR 2027 reviewer.)
- Do not reject only because the method does not beat SOTA ("not grounds for rejection by itself").
- Do not reject for minor flaws that can easily be corrected.
- Do not reject papers solely for missing citations or comparisons to work published only without review (e.g., arXiv, technical reports).
- Do not reject solely because contemporaneous work (public on or after Sep 15, 2026) reports similar ideas, methods or results, or achieves stronger performance.
- Arguments that are not grounds for rejection under the arXiv FAQ:
  - Failing to cite an arXiv paper, or failing to beat its performance, SHOULD NOT be the SOLE grounds for rejection.
  - Do not reject solely because a paper with a similar idea is already on arXiv. Report suspected plagiarism to the AC instead.
- Do not make "done before", "well known" or "common practice" claims without specific citations.
- Do not request substantial additional experiments for the rebuttal (substantial means what a major revision would need), and do not penalize a paper for lacking them (2018 PAMI-TC motion). Do not penalize a paper for supplying extra results. Clarifications, illustrations, and small experiments that can be run with academic resources during the rebuttal are allowed.
- Disregard new contributions or experiments in the rebuttal that no reviewer asked for.
- Do not require comparison on a withdrawn dataset (e.g., DukeMTMC-ReID, MS-Celeb-1M); doing so "is a violation of policy".
- Do not request comparisons to closed-source work that would need re-implementation, unless the request is justified, if it is used as a basis for the decision (2024 PAMI-TC motion). Only request comparisons to work whose publication and/or code was available "sufficiently ahead of the submission deadline".
- Do not treat the LLM Usage Statement, or the paper's LLM use itself, as a positive or negative factor.
- Do not penalize a paper for including a limitations discussion. A missing separate limitations section cannot be the sole factor for rejection.
- Do not reject solely because the paper lacks a negative-societal-impact discussion. Do not penalize a missing IRB discussion when the paper uses existing public datasets.
- Using a private or restricted dataset is not grounds for rejection, but such a dataset cannot be claimed as a contribution.
- Do not treat externally linked material (anonymous repos, Google Drive, extra videos) as part of the submission. Review only the self-contained paper and supplementary files, and flag such links to the AC.
- Do not accept or reject based on knowing or suspecting who the authors are. Do not try to discover the authors' identity, e.g. by searching the web for preprints.
- Do not show the paper, results or supplementary material to anyone, and do not use its ideas. Destroy all copies and implementations after the review.
- Do not review papers where you have a conflict of interest:
  - same institution as an author
  - collaboration in roughly the past three years
  - advisor or advisee, or the same advisor
  - involvement in the work
  - a conflict others might perceive
- Do not write belittling or sarcastic comments. Avoid "you" and "the authors"; say "the paper".
- Do not normalize to an expected acceptance rate. There is no minimum number of papers to accept or reject.
- Highly irresponsible reviews can lead to desk rejection of all the reviewer's own papers. These include:
  - unreasonably short reviews
  - generic reviews
  - reviews with factual errors that show superficial reading
  - LLM-generated reviews
  - late reviews
  - failing to acknowledge the rebuttal

## Concurrent-work policy
- Author Guidelines (authoritative): "work that first becomes publicly available on or after September 15, 2026 will generally be considered contemporaneous work." The date applies to preprints, conference papers and journal papers alike. "A submission should not be rejected solely because contemporaneous work reports similar ideas, methods, or results" (FAQ: "or achieves stronger performance"). Authors are still expected to cite and discuss relevant contemporaneous work and to provide empirical comparisons "where reasonably feasible".
- Any work that influenced the submission counts as prior work, not contemporaneous work, "regardless of when it became publicly available", and must be cited and discussed.
- Submissions unusually similar to contemporaneous work may get extra scrutiny for attribution and plagiarism. The AC judges ambiguous publication dates.
- Wording difference: the CFP says "appeared online after September 15th, 2026", while the Author Guidelines say "on or after". This profile uses the Author Guidelines.
- arXiv treatment conflicts across 2027 sources:
  - CFP: "We do not distinguish arxiv papers and other published (conference & journal) papers, and the Contemporaneous-Work rule applies in the same way."
  - Reviewer Guidelines FAQ: "Authors are not required to discuss and compare their work with recent arXiv reports, although they should properly acknowledge those that directly and obviously inspired them." Missing an arXiv citation or comparison should not be the SOLE grounds for rejection.
  - AC Guides FAQ: authors "may be excused for not knowing about papers not published in peer-reviewed conference proceedings or journals."
  - Practical reading: missing pre-Sep-15-2026 work (arXiv or published) is a legitimate weakness to raise. It can be decisive only for peer-reviewed work, or for work that directly inspired the submission.
- Dual-submission definition: a publication is peer-reviewed work longer than four pages (excluding references). arXiv preprints and technical reports are not publications. Peer-reviewed workshop papers longer than four pages are. Papers of four pages or fewer (e.g., ICIP/ICASSP) do not trigger dual-submission rejection, but the reviewer still judges whether the submission adds enough value over them.
- Closed-source comparisons: only if the work was available "sufficiently ahead of the submission deadline" (no number given).

## Score meanings
- Rating: **unconfirmed**. No 2027 scale is published. The CVPR 2026 training material used the labels "Strong Accept", "Weak Accept", "Borderline", "Weak Reject" and "Reject" for a field with a different key (`preliminary_recommendation`). That is prior-edition context only and should not be assumed for 2027.
- Confidence: **unconfirmed**.
- Venue-config decision options (not a reviewer field): "Accept (Oral)", "Accept (Poster)". The Reviewer and AC guidelines also mention spotlight and award categories, which are not in the config. ACs may also recommend papers to the Findings Track.
- AC acceptance criteria (AC Guides; context for how ratings are used):
  - Award: "major advances that will heavily impact the field; will be used by many people, create new capabilities, etc."
  - Oral: "potential to be very significant; worthwhile for the whole community to hear about."
  - Poster: "incremental steps that expand the sum of the community's knowledge or add bricks to the cathedral of knowledge; papers introducing useful tools; papers of interest to a subcommunity." Also "creative ideas that are hard to judge but could be promising -- ... give the benefit of the doubt to plausible ideas."
  - Reject: "unlikely to be significant or already known. Provide clear evidence."
- The PCs use the spread of review scores to identify borderline papers. Score distributions are not shared.

## Other policies relevant to feedback
- **LLM policy for reviewers:** see the first item under "What reviewers are told NOT to do".
  - The official OpenReview assistant (optional) can explain the paper, examine the reviewer's own strengths and weaknesses, draft from notes the reviewer endorses, and check the draft. It expects the reviewer to have read the paper and recorded an initial assessment first.
  - An LLM may be used for background research or for checking short phrases without sharing paper or review content. LLM-based grammar checkers are allowed.
  - The CFP's LLM paragraph still says the scope is "currently being finalized". That text is stale: the Reviewer and Author Guidelines contain the finalized policy.
- **Conference-side LLM checks:**
  - Reviews are checked for quality with LLM support after the preliminary deadline (correction window Jan 8-18, 2027) and again after the final deadline.
  - Correcting a flag means fixing the flagged issue, e.g. by citing paper content or giving evidence for a claim. It does not mean changing your opinion.
  - After the rebuttal, an LLM-generated summary of the reviews and rebuttal, with points of disagreement and no scores, is shared with the reviewers and the AC.
- **Prompt injection:** hidden or visible instructions meant to manipulate reviewers or automated tools count as a collusion attempt. The paper is desk-rejected, and the authors may face further sanctions. Reviewers must flag suspected injections to the AC/PCs. A reviewer who lets an injection sway an LLM-assisted review faces consequences, including desk rejection of their own papers.
- **Mandatory LLM Usage Statement (authors):** a section titled "LLM Usage Statement" after the main text and before the references. It does not count toward the page limit. Papers without it are desk-rejected. It is neutral in review.
- **Author LLM policy:** authors are responsible for all content. Papers citing non-existent material, or containing obvious factual inaccuracies, are rejected, possibly without review. Reviewers who find such inaccuracies "should act as they usually would".
- **Page limits:** 8 pages including figures and tables, plus pages containing only references and the LLM Usage Statement. Over-length, non-anonymous or wrong-template papers are rejected without review.
- **Desk-reject categories:**
  - (A) quota violations
  - (B) formatting, anonymity or administrative violations, including external links that expand content
  - (C) research-integrity violations: dual submission, plagiarism, prompt injection, fabricated content or citations
  - (D) highly irresponsible reviewing by an author
  - (E) papers that cannot be meaningfully reviewed
- **Supplementary material:** reviewers are encouraged but not obligated to read it. It may contain videos, proofs, extra figures or tables, more analysis, code, or an anonymized concurrent submission. It MAY NOT contain results from an improved version of the method or an updated or corrected PDF; report violations to the AC. Deadline: Nov 23, 2026.
- **External links:** banned in the paper, supplement and rebuttal when they expand content or subvert review (anonymous repos, Drive folders, extra videos, open-trackers). Links to the arXiv page of a cited paper, or to public third-party datasets or code not claimed as contributions, are fine.
- **Rebuttal:** a one-page PDF using the official template. Rebuttals must be anonymous, contain no links, and are subject to the prompt-injection ban. Unrequested new results are disregarded.
- **Reproducibility / code:** code is encouraged and expected (not required) for accepted papers. Checking it is optional. Keep it confidential and delete it after review.
- **Datasets:** a claimed dataset contribution must be public by camera-ready. Cite data assets like papers.
- **Ethics / IRB:** papers with human or animal subjects must name the IRB (or equivalent) and state whether consent was obtained, or explain why not (IEEE policy). Report missing statements or glaring violations to the AC/PCs. Reviewers can flag significant ethical concerns, which go to an ethics committee.
- **Findings Track:** ACs may recommend technically sound, well-validated papers with more incremental novelty to the Findings Track (separate proceedings).
- **Reciprocal reviewing (new in 2027):** each submission is expected to include an eligible reciprocal reviewer among its authors. The Responsible Reviewing and Deadline policies apply to reciprocal reviewers in full.
- **Timeline:**
  - Nov 10, 2026: abstracts due
  - Nov 16, 2026: papers due
  - Nov 23, 2026: supplementary material due
  - Dec 14, 2026: papers assigned to reviewers
  - Jan 8, 2027: reviews due
  - Jan 8-18: quality-check correction window
  - Jan 25: reviews released to authors
  - Feb 1: rebuttals due
  - Jan 25 - Feb 5: reviewer responses and discussion
  - Feb 5: final reviewer recommendations due
  - Feb 11-19: AC triplets
  - Feb 25, 2027: final decisions
  - June 22-25, 2027: conference
