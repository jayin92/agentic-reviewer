# ICCV 2025 reviewer profile

Edition choice: ICCV runs in odd years. No ICCV 2027 reviewer or author guidelines are published: those pages return 404 as of 2026-09-26. ICCV 2025 (Honolulu) is the latest edition with published reviewer guidance.

The official OpenReview review form (invitation `thecvf.com/ICCV/2025/Conference/-/Official_Review`) is not publicly readable: the API returns 403 to guests. No official ICCV 2025 page lists the form's fields, scales or option labels, and no ICCV 2025 reviewer tutorial, slides or AC guide page is published: `/Conferences/2025/ReviewerTrainingMaterial` and `/Conferences/2025/ACGuides` return 404. The form section below is pieced together from what the official pages say about the form. Anything the sources do not state is marked "unconfirmed".

## What reviewers are asked to evaluate
- Whether the paper is technically sound and contributes to the field. Look for "what is good or stimulating in the paper, and what knowledge advance it has made."
- Weigh novelty and potential impact alongside reported performance. Embrace "novel, brave concepts, even if they have not been tested on many datasets."
- Write the main critique as a list of strengths and weaknesses, explaining each argument. "Bullet lists with one short phrase per bullet are NOT a detailed review." The detailed review matters more than the score.
- Be specific about novelty. Any "done before" claim MUST cite specific references and explain how closely they are related. For a positive review, summarize the most interesting novel aspects in the Strengths section.
- Be specific about unclear writing: name the section and suggest a fix. Back up "well known" claims with specific publications.
- Give constructive feedback, such as new tools, datasets, applications or ways to generalize. If the paper is out of scope, explain why and suggest better venues. Keep an open mind unless the mismatch is extreme.
- Check reproducibility (checking code is optional), dataset-contribution claims (the dataset must be public by camera-ready), attribution of data assets (there is a review-form field for this), personal or human-subject data (IRB or ethics), discussion of negative societal impact, and discussion of limitations.
- Take time: ideally read the paper and think about it "over the course of several days" before writing.

## What reviewers are told NOT to do
- Do not reject for not beating SOTA on its own: "not grounds for rejection by itself".
- Do not reject for minor flaws that can easily be corrected.
- Do not reject papers solely for missing citations or comparisons to unreviewed prior work (e.g., arXiv, technical reports).
- Do not make "done before" or "well known" claims without specific citations.
- Arguments that are not acceptable grounds for rejection:
  - Failing to cite an arXiv paper, or failing to beat its performance, SHOULD NOT be SOLE grounds for rejection.
  - Do NOT reject solely because a paper with a similar idea already appeared on arXiv. Report suspected plagiarism to the Program Chairs.
- Do not request substantial additional experiments for the rebuttal ("Substantial" = what a major revision would need). Do not penalize a paper for lacking extra experiments, or for supplying extra results (2018 PAMI-TC motion). Small experiments that can be run with academic resources are allowed.
- Do not require comparison on a withdrawn dataset (e.g., DukeMTMC-ReID, MS-Celeb-1M). Doing so "is a violation of policy".
- Do not request comparison to closed-source work (i.e., work that requires re-implementation) without appropriate justification if it is used as a basis for the decision (2024 PAMI-TC motion). Only request comparisons to work whose publication and/or code was available sufficiently ahead of the deadline.
- Do not penalize a paper for including a limitations discussion. A missing separate limitations section cannot be the sole factor for rejection.
- Do not reject solely because the paper lacks a negative-societal-impact discussion. Do not penalize a missing IRB discussion when the paper uses existing public datasets.
- Using a private or restricted dataset is not grounds for rejection. Such a dataset cannot be claimed as a contribution.
- Do not accept or reject based on knowing or suspecting who the authors are.
- Do not try to identify the authors, e.g. by searching the web for preprints.
- Do not show the paper, results or supplementary material to anyone. Do not use the ideas yourself. Destroy all copies and implementations after the review.
- Do not review papers where you have a conflict of interest (same institution, collaboration in the past ~3 years, advisor or advisee, etc.).
- Do not write belittling or sarcastic comments. Avoid "you" and "the authors"; say "the paper".
- Do not normalize to an expected acceptance rate. There is no minimum number of papers to accept or reject.
- Highly irresponsible reviews can lead to desk rejection of all of the reviewer's own papers. These include:
  - one-sentence reviews
  - LLM-generated reviews
  - reviews irrelevant to the paper
  - reviews that overlook substantial portions of the paper
  - late reviews
- Misunderstandings, missed small details and differing opinions are explicitly not "highly irresponsible".

## Concurrent-work policy
- Not specified as a time window. No month or date threshold appears in the ICCV 2025 Reviewer Guidelines, Author Guidelines, Call for Papers or Changes page.
- What is stated (Reviewer Guidelines, arXiv FAQ): "asking authors to thoroughly compare their work with arXiv reports that appeared shortly before the submission deadline imposes an unreasonable burden." Authors "are not required to discuss and compare their work with recent arXiv reports, although they should properly acknowledge those that directly and obviously inspired them."
- Author Guidelines: a technical report or arXiv version without direct peer review "is NOT considered prior art and does NOT NEED to be cited". Authors "cannot be penalized for not citing it". Peer-reviewed works longer than four pages (excluding references) count as publications, including workshop papers. Prior papers of four pages or fewer (e.g., ICIP/ICASSP) do not trigger dual-submission rejection, but reviewers judge whether the submission adds enough value.
- Closed-source comparisons: only if the publication and/or code "has been available sufficiently ahead of the submission deadline" (no number given).
- Submission deadline for reference: Mar 7, 2025, 11:59pm HST (paper and supplementary).

## Other policies relevant to feedback
- **LLM policy for reviewers (strict prohibition):**
  - Rules: "ICCV 2025 does not allow the use of Large Language Models or online chatbots such as ChatGPT in any part of the reviewing process." They cannot be used "to write reviews or meta-reviews, whether it is run locally or via an API". Reviewers cannot use an LLM to generate content, and cannot share substantial content from the paper or the review with an LLM (e.g., no translating a review). They may use an LLM "to do background research or to check short phrases for clarity". LLM-based grammar checkers are allowed.
  - Enforcement (Changes page): ACs monitor for violations. The process "will not aim to definitively determine whether a review was written by an LLM … but rather to assess whether the review is highly irresponsible". If it is, the reviewer's own papers may be desk-rejected.
- **Prompt injection:** not addressed in the ICCV 2025 reviewer or author guidelines (the policy first appears in CVPR 2026).
- **Responsible Reviewing and Deadline policies:** highly irresponsible or late reviews can lead to desk rejection of all papers the reviewer authored, at PC discretion. Review data, including reviewer PII and quality and timeliness statistics, is shared privately with PCs of future venues.
- **Reviewer identity:** after final decisions, reviewer names become visible to the other reviewers of the same paper. ACs and PCs always see them.
- **Page limits:** 8 pages including figures and tables, plus unlimited reference pages. Over-length, non-anonymized or wrong-template papers are rejected without review. Each author may submit at most 25 papers.
- **Supplementary material:** reviewers are encouraged but not obligated to read it. It may include videos, proofs, extra figures or tables, more analysis, code, or an anonymized concurrent submission. It MAY NOT contain results from an improved version of the method or an updated or corrected paper PDF. Report violations to the AC. The supplementary deadline was the same as the paper deadline (Mar 7, 2025).
- **Rebuttal:** one-page PDF using the official template. Longer rebuttals are not reviewed. Rebuttals must be anonymous and contain no links to external material. Authors should not add new contributions or experiments that reviewers did not request, and reviewers are "instructed to disregard any such contributions". Authors may send a confidential comment to the AC.
- **Reproducibility / code:** code submission is encouraged, not required. Checking it is optional. Keep code confidential and delete it after review.
- **Limitations and societal impact:** weigh an honest discussion POSITIVELY. Neither is formally required.
- **Ethics:** reviewers can flag significant ethical concerns, which go to an ethics committee. For papers using personal or human-subject data without an IRB or ethics statement, inform the ACs/PCs and do not handle it yourself.
- **Author LLM policy (context):** authors may use LLMs but are responsible for the content. Citations to non-existent material or obvious factual inaccuracies lead to rejection, possibly without review. Reviewers "should act as they usually would".
- **Timeline:** papers assigned Mar 27, 2025; reviews due Apr 23 (strict); released May 9; rebuttal deadline May 16; final ratings due May 27; decisions to authors Jun 25, 2025. The CFP says "June 26th".
- **Process:** 3 reviewers per paper; each reviewer typically handles 5-8 papers; ACs handle up to ~30 papers each; ACs work in AC triplets.
