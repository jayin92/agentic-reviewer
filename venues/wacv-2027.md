# WACV 2027 reviewer profile

Two-round process (Author Guidelines, Reviewer Guidelines, AC Guides):
- **Round 1**: submission June 26, 2026; papers to reviewers July 3; reviews due July 24; AC decisions/meta-reviews due Aug 4; reviews and decisions to authors Aug 7, 2026 (the Dates page says Aug 9). Rebuttal + revision due Aug 28; final decisions Oct 9, 2026.
- **Round 2**: submission Aug 28, 2026; papers to reviewers Sept 4; reviews due Sept 25; AC decisions due Oct 6; reviews and final decisions to authors Oct 9, 2026. **No rebuttal or revision in Round 2.**
- Conference: Jan 4–8, 2027. Reviewers get about 3–4 papers per round, about 6–7 in total.

## What reviewers are asked to evaluate
- Apply the criteria for the paper's **track** (declared by the authors): Applications, Algorithms, or Evaluations & Datasets (E&D is new for 2027).
  - **Applications**: "must be evaluated on systems-level innovation, novelty of the domain and comparative assessment. They should not be evaluated solely on the basis of algorithmic novelty (i.e, it is okay to have algorithmic novelty, but it is also okay not to have it)." Systems-level innovation examples: (1) a new task; (2) a new formulation of an existing application; (3) a new way of benchmarking existing methods.
  - **Algorithms**: "must be evaluated according to the standard conference criteria including algorithmic novelty and quantified evaluation against current, alternative approaches. They should not be evaluated solely on the basis of systems-level innovation." Similar in style to CVPR/ICCV/ECCV.
  - **Evaluations & Datasets**: "must be evaluated similarly to NeurIPS Evaluations & Dataset Track." Covers tools, datasets, benchmarks, and practices for testing, stress-testing, auditing, comparing, and interpreting AI/ML systems. Ask whether the dataset "will meaningfully advance any particular sub-areas in Computer Vision, and if the proposed dataset and benchmarking efforts are scientifically correct."
- Each accepted paper "should be technically sound and make an application or algorithmic contribution to the field." Look for what is good or stimulating, and what new application or knowledge advancement it makes. Embrace "novel, brave applications and concepts, even if they have not been tested on many datasets"; weigh novelty and potential impact alongside performance.
- Write the main critique as a list of strengths and weaknesses with explained arguments. "A single short sentence or a few words do NOT suffice." Include specific feedback for improvement.
- Be specific about novelty. "Has been done before" claims MUST cite specific references and explain how close they are. For related-work claims, "You MUST cite specific publications"; otherwise ACs discount the claim.
- Check reproducibility: optionally check submitted code. Reviewers are "encouraged to use the Reproducibility Checklist" (the Author Guidelines say reviewers are "encouraged to check the submitted code").
- Check attribution of data assets and "comment in the corresponding field in the review form."
- Check use of personal data and human subjects: IRB clearance or a description of the ethical principles followed. Report glaring violations to ACs/PCs; do not handle them directly.
- Be specific about writing problems. If the paper is out of scope, explain why and suggest better venues, but keep an open mind.
- For Round 1 revisions: "The revised version will be sent to the same reviewers and ACs, after which the final decisions will be made." What reviewers submit at that stage is unconfirmed.

## What reviewers are told NOT to do
- Do not reject solely because the method does not exceed SOTA on an existing benchmark ("not grounds for rejection by itself").
- Do not reject for minor flaws that can be easily corrected.
- Do not judge Application papers solely on algorithmic novelty, or Algorithms papers solely on systems-level innovation. The AC Guides warn that reviewers have wrongly recommended rejecting application papers for lacking algorithmic novelty, and ACs may overrule them.
- Do not reject papers solely because they miss citations or comparisons to unreviewed prior work (arXiv, tech reports). Failing to cite an arXiv paper or beat its performance "SHOULD NOT be SOLE grounds for rejection". Do not reject solely because a similar idea appeared on arXiv.
- Do not require comparison on a withdrawn dataset "without a detailed consultation with PCs" (policy violation).
- Do not penalize a paper for omitting an IRB discussion when it uses an existing public dataset.
- Do not try to discover author identities ("DO NOT attempt to discover them by searching the Web for preprints"). Do not accept or reject based on known or suspected authorship.
- Do not submit highly irresponsible reviews: one-sentence reviews, LLM-generated reviews, irrelevant reviews, or reviews that overlook substantial portions of the paper.
- Do not show results, videos, images, code, or supplementary material to non-reviewers. Do not use ideas or code from reviewed papers. You must acknowledge any colleague or student who helped write a review.
- Do not give away your identity by asking the authors to cite several of your own papers.
- Do not make belittling or sarcastic comments. Avoid second person ("you") and preferably "the authors"; use "the paper". Use neutral pronouns.
- Do not assume your stack should match the conference acceptance rate.

## Concurrent-work policy
No numeric cut-off (e.g., "N months before the deadline") is specified. Verbatim from the Reviewer FAQ: "Consistent with good academic practice, the authors should cite all sources that inspired and informed their work. This said, asking authors to thoroughly compare their work with arXiv reports that appeared shortly before the submission deadline imposes an unreasonable burden. We also do not wish to discourage the publication of similar ideas that have been developed independently and concurrently." It then lists:
- "Authors are not required to discuss and compare their work with recent arXiv reports, although they should properly acknowledge those that directly and obviously inspired them."
- "Failing to cite an arXiv paper or failing to beat its performance SHOULD NOT be SOLE grounds for rejection."
- "Reviewers SHOULD NOT reject a paper solely because another paper with a similar idea has already appeared on arXiv. If the reviewer suspects plagiarism or academic dishonesty, they are encouraged to bring these concerns to the attention of the Program Chairs."
- "It is acceptable for a reviewer to suggest that an author should acknowledge or be aware of something on arXiv."

Related definitions from the Author Guidelines:
- A "publication" is "a written work longer than four pages (excluding references) that was submitted for review by peers for either acceptance or rejection, and, after review, was accepted." arXiv preprints and technical reports are not publications.
- "a technical report (departmental, arXiv, etc.) version of the submission that is put up without any form of direct peer-review is NOT considered prior art and does NOT NEED to be cited in the submission; authors may cite such material, but cannot be penalized for not citing it."
- Dual submission: "substantially similar in content (defined as having 20 percent or more overlap)".

## Score meanings
- Reviewer recommendation (Round 1 and Round 2): unconfirmed. The number of levels and their labels are not published in any official WACV 2027 source.
- Confidence: unconfirmed.
- **AC decision options, Round 1** (Author Guidelines, verbatim):
  - "Accept: The submission has been accepted to WACV 2027."
  - "Revise and Resubmit: The submission has not been accepted to WACV 2027, but may be made acceptable after some revisions. If the submission is invited for resubmission, a revised version and a rebuttal can be uploaded. The authors may choose to withdraw the paper as well."
  - "Reject: The submission has been rejected from WACV 2027. ACs will make this recommendation when the reviews raise substantial revisions required that cannot be reasonably addressed in a short time frame. Rejected round 1 papers which are submitted to round 2 as new papers will be desk rejected."
- **AC decision options, Round 1 revisions and new Round 2 submissions**:
  - "Accept: The submission has been accepted to WACV 2027."
  - "Reject: The submission has been rejected from WACV 2027."
- Revised Round 1 submissions include a revised paper (required), a 1-page rebuttal on the provided template that addresses the reviewers and highlights the changes (required), and revised supplementary material (optional).
- Presentation format: the OpenReview accept decision options are "Accept (Oral)" and "Accept (Poster)". The SAC Guide says AC pairs recommend "oral, spotlight, poster" formats if WACV 2027 has multiple presentation styles.

## Other policies relevant to feedback
- **LLM policy for reviewers.** The only reviewer-side statement is in the Responsible Reviewing Policy: "reviews generated by Large Language Models (LLMs)" are an example of highly irresponsible behavior. Any reviewer whose review is deemed highly irresponsible "will face a desk rejection of all papers on which they are an author per the discretion of the PCs." The following are not specified in the WACV 2027 guidelines (unconfirmed): whether limited uses such as grammar checking or background research are allowed, and whether sharing paper content with an LLM is explicitly banned.
- **Author-side LLM use.** Authors follow the NeurIPS 2025 policy, slightly modified: LLM use that matters to the methodology must be described, and authors are responsible for all content. Citations to non-existent material lead to rejection, possibly without review. The SAC Guide says ACs flag "papers with significant LLM usage not disclosed in the paper".
- **Reviewing deadline policy.** The deadline is strictly enforced; late reviewers risk desk rejection of their own papers.
- **Rebuttal (Round 1 only).** One-page PDF on the WACV template, anonymous, and no links (even anonymous ones). Authors may send a confidential comment to the AC. Round 2 has no rebuttal.
- **Supplementary material.** Reviewers are encouraged but not obligated to read it. It may not include "results on additional datasets, results obtained with an improved version of the method ... or an updated or corrected version of the submission PDF". Report violations to the PCs. Code must be anonymized; if it reveals identity, notify the PCs and review as if it were anonymized.
- **Page limit.** Eight pages (excluding references) in WACV style. Non-anonymized papers, papers not using the template, and papers over the limit are rejected without review.
- **Datasets.** A dataset claimed as a contribution must be available by camera-ready. Non-public datasets may be used but not claimed as contributions.
- **Anonymity.** arXiv posting, project websites, and video links are allowed. Reviewers must not search for author identities.
- **Ethics.** Reviewers can flag significant ethical concerns for PC review. Negative societal impact should be discussed when significant; the guidelines are adapted from ICCV 2025.
- **Confidentiality.** Protect ideas and destroy papers, supplementary material, and code after review. Conflicts include the same institution, a collaboration within about 3 years, and advisor/advisee (a lifetime conflict).
