# CVPR 2026 reviewer profile

Edition choice: CVPR 2027 has a Call for Papers (deadline Nov 16, 2026), but no reviewer guidelines, author guidelines, AC guides or training material (those pages return 404 as of 2026-09-26). CVPR 2026 is the latest edition with published reviewer guidance.

The official OpenReview review form (invitation `thecvf.com/CVPR/2026/Conference/-/Official_Review`) is not publicly readable: the API returns 403 to guests. No official page lists the form's fields, scales or option labels. The form section below is pieced together from what the official guideline pages say about the form. Anything the sources do not state is marked "unconfirmed".

## What reviewers are asked to evaluate
- Whether the paper is technically sound and contributes to the field. Look for "what is good or stimulating in the paper, and what knowledge advance it has made."
- Weigh novelty and potential impact together with reported performance. Embrace "novel, brave concepts, even if they have not been tested on many datasets."
- Recommend acceptance for papers that "will interest CVPR attendees, are technically sound, and contribute meaningfully (methodologically, empirically, or conceptually)". Balance novelty against significance and potential impact (training material).
- Write the main critique as a list of strengths and weaknesses, explaining each argument. "Bullet lists with one short phrase per bullet are NOT a detailed review."
- Be specific about novelty. Any "done before" claim MUST cite specific references and explain how closely they are related. For a positive review, summarize the most interesting novel aspects in the Strengths section.
- Be specific about writing problems: name the unclear section and suggest how to fix it.
- Give constructive feedback, such as new tools, datasets, applications or ways to generalize. If the paper is out of scope, explain why and suggest better venues. Keep an open mind unless the mismatch is extreme.
- Check reproducibility (checking code is optional), dataset-contribution claims (the dataset must be public by camera-ready), attribution of data assets (there is a review-form field for this), use of personal or human-subject data (IRB or ethics), discussion of negative societal impact, and discussion of limitations.
- Training material, recommended review structure: a concise summary (2-4 sentences), strengths, weaknesses, constructive suggestions, and a justified recommendation that ties the assessment to the rating.
- Training material, final justification: it must be specific, evidence-based, tied to the rating, and must explain what the rebuttal changed or did not change. The training material gives examples of unacceptable justifications, e.g. "The rebuttal addresses my concerns, so I increase my score."

## What reviewers are told NOT to do
- Do not reject for not beating SOTA on its own: "not grounds for rejection by itself". Do not over-weight SOTA tables. Do not reason "Beats SOTA ⇒ accept" / "Fails SOTA ⇒ reject".
- Do not reject for minor flaws that can easily be corrected.
- Do not reject papers solely for missing citations or comparisons to unreviewed prior work (e.g., arXiv, technical reports).
- Do not make "done before", "well known" or "common practice in industry" claims without specific citations.
- Arguments that are not acceptable grounds for rejection:
  - Failing to cite an arXiv paper, or failing to beat its performance, SHOULD NOT be the SOLE grounds for rejection.
  - Do NOT reject solely because a paper with a similar idea already appeared on arXiv. Report suspected plagiarism to the AC instead.
- Do not request substantial additional experiments for the rebuttal ("Substantial" = what a major revision would need). Do not penalize a paper for lacking extra experiments, or for supplying extra results. Small experiments that can be run with academic resources during rebuttal are allowed.
- Do not require comparison on a withdrawn dataset (e.g., DukeMTMC-ReID, MS-Celeb-1M). Doing so "is a violation of policy".
- Do not request comparison to closed-source work (i.e., work that requires re-implementation) without appropriate justification if it is used as a basis for the decision (2024 PAMI-TC motion). Only request comparisons to work whose publication and/or code was available sufficiently ahead of the deadline.
- Do not penalize a paper for including a limitations discussion. A missing separate limitations section cannot be the sole factor for rejection.
- Do not reject solely because the paper lacks a negative-societal-impact discussion. Do not penalize a missing IRB discussion when the paper uses existing public datasets.
- Using a private or restricted dataset is not grounds for rejection. Such a dataset cannot be claimed as a contribution.
- Do not accept or reject based on knowing or suspecting who the authors are.
- Do not try to find the authors' identity, e.g. by searching the web for preprints or by actively searching arXiv.
- Do not show the paper, results or supplementary material to anyone. Do not use the ideas yourself. Destroy all copies and implementations after the review.
- Do not review papers where you have a conflict of interest (same institution, collaboration in the past ~3 years, advisor or advisee, etc.).
- Do not write belittling or sarcastic comments. Avoid "you" and "the authors"; say "the paper".
- Do not normalize to an expected acceptance rate. There is no minimum number of papers to accept or reject.
- Irresponsible reviews can lead to desk rejection of all of the reviewer's own papers. These include:
  - unreasonably short reviews
  - generic reviews
  - reviews with factual errors that show superficial reading
  - LLM-generated or LLM-assisted reviews
  - late reviews
- Training material, "standard errors" to avoid: ignorance or inaccuracy, pure opinion, the novelty fallacy, blank assertions, "policy entrepreneurism" (inventing requirements not in policy, e.g. "must release code", "must beat SOTA on all benchmarks") and intellectual laziness.
- Training material: evaluate rebuttals on what they actually show. "Promises without evidence should not change your assessment."

## Concurrent-work policy
- CVPR 2026 AC Guides FAQ: "We consider papers contemporaneous if they are published within the last four months." The phrase "within the last four months" is not tied to a stated reference date. Presumably it is measured from the submission deadline (Nov 13, 2025), but the reference date is unconfirmed.
- Reviewer Guidelines (arXiv FAQ): authors "are not required to discuss and compare their work with recent arXiv reports, although they should properly acknowledge those that directly and obviously inspired them". The Guidelines give no month threshold, only "arXiv reports that appeared shortly before the submission deadline".
- Author Guidelines: a technical report or arXiv version put up without direct peer review "is NOT considered prior art and does NOT NEED to be cited". Authors "cannot be penalized for not citing it". Peer-reviewed papers longer than four pages (excluding references) count as publications, including workshop papers. Papers of four pages or fewer (e.g., ICIP/ICASSP) do not trigger dual-submission rejection. Reviewers still judge whether the submission adds enough value over them.
- Closed-source comparisons: only if the publication and/or code "has been available sufficiently ahead of the submission deadline" (no number given).
- For reference only, since it applies to a different edition: the CVPR 2027 CFP defines as contemporaneous papers that appeared online after Sep 15, 2026, i.e. about two months before its Nov 16, 2026 deadline. This does not apply to CVPR 2026.

## Score meanings
- Preliminary / final recommendation: **unconfirmed**. The only official wording is the example set of final justifications in the Reviewer Training Material, headed "Strong Accept", "Weak Accept", "Borderline", "Weak Reject" and "Reject". The Borderline example says: "it would strengthen acceptance if published but wouldn't be a major loss if rejected." Numeric values, whether other levels exist (e.g., a separate "Strong Reject" or "Borderline Accept/Reject"), and the exact option text are unconfirmed.
- Confidence: **unconfirmed**. No official scale is published.
- Final decision categories (venue config, not a reviewer field): "Accept (Oral)", "Accept (Highlight)", "Accept (Poster)". Papers can also be recommended to the Findings track, which ACs do.
- AC acceptance criteria (AC Guides; context for how scores are used):
  - Award: "major advances that will heavily impact the field"
  - Oral: "potential to be very significant; worthwhile for the whole community to hear about"
  - Poster: "incremental steps that expand the sum of the community's knowledge… useful tools… of interest to a subcommunity", plus "creative ideas that are hard to judge but could be promising"
  - Reject: "unlikely to be significant or already known. Provide clear evidence."

## Other policies relevant to feedback
- **LLM policy for reviewers (strict prohibition):**
  - Rules: "CVPR 2026 does not allow the use of Large Language Models or online chatbots such as ChatGPT in any part of the reviewing process." LLMs "are NOT allowed to be used to write reviews or meta-reviews, whether it is run locally or via an API." Reviewers cannot use an LLM to generate content, and cannot share substantial content from the paper or the review with an LLM (e.g., no translating a review). They may use an LLM "to do background research or to check short phrases for clarity". LLM-based grammar checkers are allowed.
  - Training material (stricter wording): LLMs must not "generate, translate, paraphrase, or otherwise compose review content". Sharing "any part of any submission (the paper, quotes from the paper, captions, figures, etc.)" with an LLM is prohibited. Grammar checks are limited to short phrases "under ~50 words, containing no submission-specific content". Background research is allowed only on public concepts, not on the submission.
  - Enforcement: reviews are checked for LLM use. A violation enters the irresponsible-review oversight process and can lead to desk rejection of the reviewer's own papers. The training material also says violators "may be barred from submitting to CVPR for two years".
- **Prompt injection:** hidden instructions in a submission are treated as a collusion attempt and an ethics violation. Authors can be desk-rejected. Reviewers must flag suspected injections to the AC/PCs. A reviewer who lets an injection sway an LLM-generated review "may face consequences, including the desk rejection of their own submissions".
- **Responsible Reviewing and Deadline policies:** highly irresponsible or late reviews can lead to desk rejection of all papers the reviewer authored, at PC discretion. Review-quality and timeliness statistics, with reviewer OpenReview IDs, are shared privately with PCs of future venues.
- **Page limits:** 8 pages including figures and tables, plus unlimited reference pages. Over-length, non-anonymized or wrong-template papers are rejected without review.
- **Supplementary material:** reviewers are encouraged but not obligated to read it. It may include videos, proofs, extra figures or tables, more analysis, code, or an anonymized concurrent submission. It MAY NOT contain results from an improved version of the method or an updated or corrected paper PDF. Report violations to the AC. The supplementary deadline was Nov 20, 2025, one week after the paper deadline.
- **External links:** links that expand content or subvert review are banned in the paper, supplement and rebuttal.
- **Rebuttal:** one-page PDF using the official template. Longer rebuttals are not reviewed. Rebuttals must be anonymous and contain no external links. Authors should not add new contributions or experiments that reviewers did not request, and reviewers are "instructed to disregard any such contributions". Authors may also send a confidential comment to the AC.
- **Reproducibility / code:** code submission is encouraged, not required. Checking it is optional. Keep code confidential and delete it after review.
- **Limitations and societal impact:** weigh an honest discussion POSITIVELY. Neither is formally required.
- **Ethics:** reviewers can flag significant ethical concerns, which go to an ethics committee. For papers using personal or human-subject data without an IRB or ethics statement, inform the AC/PCs and do not handle it yourself.
- **Author LLM policy (context):** authors may use LLMs but are responsible for the content. Citations to non-existent material or obvious factual inaccuracies lead to rejection, possibly without review. Reviewers who find such inaccuracies "should act as they usually would".
- **Compute reporting:** an experimental author compute report. It is "not visible to reviewers and will not influence acceptance decisions".
- **Findings track:** ACs may recommend technically sound papers with solid experiments but incremental novelty to the Findings track (workshop proceedings).
- **Timeline:** reviews due Jan 12, 2026; released to authors Jan 22; rebuttals due Jan 29; AC-reviewer discussion Jan 30 - Feb 5; final reviewer recommendations due Feb 5; decisions Feb 20, 2026.
- **Time budget (training material):** about 4-6 hours per paper in total.
