# Eurographics 2027 reviewer profile

Source tags used below: [EG27] = EG 2027 CFP or EG 2027 SRMv2 instructions; [EG26] = EG 2026 CFP (unconfirmed for 2027); [EG25-preface] = EG 2025 CGF preface (unconfirmed for 2027); [EG-guidelines] = eg.org CGF/EG Publications reviewers' guidelines (general, applies to all EG publications, no edition).

Timeline [EG27]: abstract and submission form due Fri 25 Sep 2026; full paper due Thu 1 Oct 2026 (both 23:59 AoE after extension); reviews released Mon 23 Nov 2026; rebuttal due Mon 30 Nov 2026; conditional acceptance/rejection Fri 18 Dec 2026; revised version due Tue 26 Jan 2027; final notification Tue 9 Feb 2027; camera-ready Tue 23 Feb 2027. Review system: SRMv2. Full Papers chairs: Elena Garces and Amir Vaxman.

Process: "double-blind two-step review process" and "Each paper receives five quality reviews" [EG27]. Step 1 is review, rebuttal, then conditional acceptance or rejection. Step 2 reviews the revised version of conditionally accepted papers before final acceptance [EG27]. In 2025, a sorting committee assigned each paper to two IPC members (primary and secondary), who each invited tertiary reviewers, three per paper in total. Review was double-blind for tertiaries and single-blind for the IPC. Decisions came out of a one-week virtual asynchronous IPC meeting. The second-round check of minor revisions was done by the primary (sometimes also the secondary) reviewer. Some papers were sent to a fast-track major-revision process for a regular CGF issue. The acceptance rate was 36.6% (75/205) [EG25-preface]. Whether all of this holds in 2027 is unconfirmed.

## What reviewers are asked to evaluate
EG publishes no detailed evaluation rubric. The criteria below are what the CFP and guidelines actually say.
- **Originality and impact.** The program seeks "innovative research" and "new ideas", encouraging "all forms of research, creativity, and originality, with the ambition of setting the standard in the field and stimulating future trends" [EG27].
- **Scope.** All areas of computer graphics, "including but not limited to: rendering, modeling, animation, generative AI, deep learning for graphics, simulation, geometry processing, image/video editing, fabrication, 3D printing, computational imaging, display technologies, graphics hardware, human-computer interaction, visualization, virtual and augmented reality" [EG27]. Besides methods and algorithms, EG explicitly welcomes "new datasets and benchmarks" and "original perceptual and experimental studies" [EG27].
- **Engagement with the graphics literature.** Papers "should, as a rule of thumb, engage meaningfully with the existing Computer Graphics literature and situate their contribution within it. Submissions that make little or no connection to this body of work may fall outside the scope of EUROGRAPHICS and could be desk-rejected" [EG27].
- **Prior art and comparisons.** "Authors are expected to cite, discuss differences and novelty, and compare results, if applicable, with respect to relevant existing publications, provided they have been published in a peer-reviewed venue. This also applies to patents" [EG27]. Reviewers should therefore check citation, discussion of differences, and (where applicable) result comparison against peer-reviewed prior work and patents.
- **Proper crediting / plagiarism.** Sources to be credited are "not limited to peer-reviewed publications but also include patents, textbooks, technical reports, theses, unpublished work posted on arXiv, and other posts on the World Wide Web". Failure to credit is plagiarism and leads to rejection [EG27]. Authors must cite non-peer-reviewed works "that inspired them" [EG27].
- **Concision and length.** There is no maximum length. About 10 pages (CGF style, figures included, references excluded) is recommended for research papers and about 20 for surveys. "Reviewers might rank submissions perceived as being either repetitive or unnecessarily long lower than they would score concisely written papers" [EG27].
- **Supplementary material.** Typical supplements are videos, high-resolution images, appendices, raw or processed data, and source code, all anonymized. "Reviewers will be encouraged to look at it" [EG27].
- **Replicability.** EG's publication ethics statement asks authors to warrant that "any formula is accurate", and that "in order to permit others to replicate the work, the manuscript should contain accurate underlying data, sufficient detail and all the necessary references" [EG-guidelines]. The Graphics Replicability Stamp is optional recognition, not a requirement [EG27].
- **A careful, useful review.** "You should make an effort to do a good review", which means reading the paper carefully. "The most valuable comments in a review are those that help the authors understand the shortcomings of their work and how they might improve it" [EG-guidelines].
- **Human-subjects data** [EG26, unconfirmed for 2027]: there should be IRB approval, or else a discussion of pending approval, how the data was obtained and how consent was obtained, in the paper or the supplement.
- **Reference integrity** [EG26, unconfirmed for 2027]: "Papers containing citations of non-existent material or obvious factual inaccuracies will be rejected when found, and may be rejected without review." Flag any reference that cannot be verified.

## What reviewers are told NOT to do
- **Do not use LLMs to write reviews.** "Large language models (LLMs) are NOT allowed to be used for writing reviews at any step. This is true for any LLM, whether you run it locally or use an API." You cannot ask an LLM to write content, and you cannot share substantial paper or review content with one, so no LLM translation either. An LLM may be used only "to do background research or to check short phrases for clarity". Grammar checkers are allowed [EG-guidelines]. Any AI-drafted text is therefore only input for the human reviewer, who must write the submitted review in their own words and from their own judgment.
- **Do not breach confidentiality.** Do not show the paper, videos or other materials to non-reviewers. Do not use ideas from reviewed papers. After the review, destroy all copies and any implementations or results made to evaluate the paper [EG-guidelines].
- **Do not review with a conflict of interest.** See the conflict rules under Other policies [EG-guidelines].
- **Do not write casual, flippant or sketchy reviews.** "A casual or flippant review of a paper that the author has seriously submitted is not appropriate and discredits the entire Eurographics review process." If you cannot do a decent job, back out early [EG-guidelines].
- **Do not belittle or use sarcasm.** "Belittling or sarcastic comments may help display one's wit, but they are unnecessary in the reviewing process ... If you intensely dislike a paper, give it a low score. That makes a sufficient statement" [EG-guidelines].
- **Do not demand comparisons with non-peer-reviewed prepublications.** Authors "are not required to discuss and compare their work with recent non-peer-reviewed prepublications (arXiv, technical reports, theses, etc.)". If a reviewer points out a missed arXiv work, conditionally accepted authors only cite it as concurrent work, "without the burden of having to detail how their work compares to or differs from" it [EG27].
- **Do not penalize public preprints.** Archiving the submission on arXiv or as a tech report, before or after the deadline, is allowed. So are anonymous code repositories and unlisted videos [EG27]. "There is no penalty for publishing a submission as a prepublication" [EG26]. Authors must not cite their own largely similar prepublication. It goes in a "Prepublications" form field that is visible to the IPC but not to reviewers [EG27].
- **Do not expect new results in the rebuttal** [EG26, unconfirmed for 2027]. The 2026 CFP disallows "New results, even if they strengthen your claims", "Major revisions or additions to the methodology", and "Extending the scope of the paper beyond what was originally submitted". The rebuttal is for clarifications, answers to reviewer questions, pointing out factual errors, and rephrasing existing content. "Reviewers base their decisions on the original submission." For 2027 the CFP only says the rebuttal is "a brief document to address any factual errors or clarify any issues raised by reviews" [EG27]. Questions for the rebuttal should therefore be answerable from the submitted work.
- EG does **not** publish SIGGRAPH-style rules about which comparisons may be demanded (public code, compute resources, user studies). There is no EG rule to cite either way. Reviewers should weigh such requests sensibly, but no venue rule restricts them.

## Concurrent-work policy
- **Peer-reviewed publications and patents** are prior art without any stated recency exemption. Authors are expected to cite them, discuss differences and novelty, and compare results where applicable [EG27]. **No time window is specified.** EG has no "N weeks or months before the deadline" rule like SIGGRAPH/SIGGRAPH Asia (4 weeks) or CVPR.
- **Non-peer-reviewed prepublications** (arXiv, technical reports, theses, etc.): authors are not required to discuss or compare with "recent" ones. They must still cite those "that inspired them", and they are encouraged to mention all related work they know of. Missed arXiv works that reviewers point out must be cited "as concurrent work" in the final revision of conditionally accepted papers, without detailed comparison [EG27].
- **Authors' own largely similar prepublications or posters** are not cited in the paper. They are listed in the "Prepublications" field, which reviewers cannot see [EG27].

## Score meanings
- Overall Recommendation [EG26; 2027 unconfirmed]: 1 "very poor"; 2 "poor"; 3 "clearly below EG standard"; 4 "dubious - not quite acceptable"; 5 "marginal - only just acceptable"; 6 "acceptable"; 7 "good"; 8 "very good"; 9 "excellent". "Please also note that the textual descriptions are merely orientative." So 5 is the lowest score labelled acceptable, and 4 or below is not acceptable.
- Decision outcomes after round 1 [EG27]: conditional acceptance (revise for a second-round review) or rejection. In 2025, some papers were instead "recommended to a fast-track review process with major revisions" for a future CGF issue [EG25-preface].
- No other score or choice fields are publicly documented.

## Other policies relevant to feedback
- **Rebuttal**: a "brief document to address any factual errors or clarify any issues raised by reviews", due one week after the reviews are released [EG27]. In 2025 it was a "1000-word rebuttal" [EG25-preface]; the 2027 length is unconfirmed. In 2025, all reviewers of a paper read the rebuttal and all reviews, then reached an initial decision together [EG25-preface].
- **Second round**: conditionally accepted papers submit a revised version (due 26 Jan 2027), which is reviewed again before final notification [EG27]. (Reviewer-practice inference, not an EG rule: because of the second round, it helps to separate must-fix items from optional suggestions.)
- **Re-submitted material**: authors of papers previously rejected or withdrawn elsewhere are encouraged, not required, to upload a cover letter as an "Additional Attachment" that describes the paper's history and answers the prior reviews. This "does not imply reviewer continuity" [EG27].
- **Double submission**: claimed contributions must not be published, accepted or under review elsewhere in substantially similar form. Violations are grounds for desk rejection [EG27].
- **Anonymity**: double-blind toward reviewers. Own work is cited in the third person. Authors must not publicize the work via institutional PR or webpages or identify themselves in repositories [EG27]. If a reviewer recognizes the work or author and a conflict could arise, they notify the senior reviewer [EG-guidelines].
- **Conflicts of interest** [EG-guidelines]: recuse yourself if you are at the same institution; were directly involved or are receiving credit (including membership of the thesis committee for the thesis work); others might perceive a conflict; collaborated (co-authored) with an author in the past two years; or were the PhD advisor or advisee of an author (a lifetime conflict).
- **Author GenAI use**: "If an author used a GenAI tool to develop any portion of a manuscript, its use must be described, transparently and in detail, in the AI Use Declaration Statement during submission" [EG27], following CGF rules. Under the general EG author rules, authors bear full responsibility and AI cannot be an author [EG-guidelines]. Whether reviewers see the declaration is unconfirmed.
- **Page limits / format**: CGF LaTeX submission template. There is no hard page limit, about 10 pages is recommended for research papers (about 20 for surveys), and the upload limit is 500 MB [EG27].
- **Code/data**: encouraged as supplementary material, but not required. There is no reproducibility checklist. The Replicability Stamp is optional [EG27].
- **Limitations section**: no requirement is specified.
- **Ethics review**: EG follows COPE publication ethics [EG-guidelines]. No separate ethics-review track is described. Human-subjects/IRB guidance appears only in EG 2026 [EG26].
- **Presentation**: at least one author must register and present in person [EG27]. This is not a review criterion.
- **Tone**: be serious, thorough and professional. Do not include sarcasm. Constructive comments that help authors improve are the most valuable [EG-guidelines].
