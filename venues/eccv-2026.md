# ECCV 2026 reviewer profile

Timeline (Reviewer Guide): papers to reviewers Mar 31, 2026; reviews due Apr 21, 2026 (strict); rebuttals to reviewers May 12; discussion May 12–20; final reviewer recommendations due May 20, 2026. Final decisions to authors June 17, 2026 (AC Guidelines).

## What reviewers are asked to evaluate
- Each accepted paper "should be technically sound and make a contribution to the field." Look for what is good or stimulating and what knowledge advancement it makes. Embrace "novel, brave concepts, even if they have not been tested on many datasets"; weigh novelty and potential impact alongside reported performance.
- NEW in 2026: each paper declares one primary **Contribution Type**: Algorithms/General (default), Theory/Foundational, Applied/Systems, Datasets/Benchmarks, Concept & Feasibility. Reviewers must evaluate the paper "primarily through the lens of" that declared type, using the type-specific guidance as a framework, not a rigid checklist. Contribution Types are not separate tracks.
  - Algorithms/General: novelty and contribution, technical soundness (incl. ablations), empirical evaluation (fair baselines, reproducible, statistically meaningful, limitations acknowledged), generality and impact. Do not penalize for lack of new datasets, absence of deployment, limited theory.
  - Theory/Foundational: significance of the theory, rigor and correctness, relevance to CV; experiments are illustrative. Do not penalize for no SOTA, limited empirical scale, no new algorithm.
  - Applied/Systems: problem relevance, system design and engineering quality, real-world impact, evaluation; any new method judged as part of the system. Do not penalize for limited methodological novelty or not advancing theory.
  - Datasets/Benchmarks: significance, data quality and design (incl. ethics/consent/bias), accessibility and documentation (must be public by camera-ready; non-public parts are not a contribution), benchmark setup and baselines; any method judged only as a baseline. Do not penalize for limited methodological novelty or lower SOTA on unrelated benchmarks.
  - Concept & Feasibility: originality and vision, correctness, claim–evidence alignment, quality of feasibility validation, clarity and framing. Do not penalize for lack of large-scale experiments, no SOTA, non-exhaustive ablations, limited deployment/benchmarking.
- If the reviewer disagrees with the declared Contribution Type, they may evaluate under another type only if they explicitly explain why, which type fits better, and how that changed the evaluation, in the "Justification of Rating" section.
- Main critique should be a list of strengths and weaknesses with explained arguments; "Bullet lists with one short phrase per bullet are NOT a detailed review." Discussion matters more than the score.
- Be specific about novelty: claims that work "has been done before" MUST cite specific references and explain how closely related; the same applies to positive claims. Be specific about unclear writing (point to sections, suggest fixes).
- Give constructive feedback for improvement (new tools, datasets, applications, generalizations). If out of scope, explain why and suggest venues, but keep an open mind unless the mismatch is extreme.
- Check: reproducibility (code optional to check), attribution of data assets (datasets/code must be cited), personal data / human subjects (IRB or ethical principles described; escalate glaring violations to ACs/PCs rather than handling directly), negative societal impact discussion (weigh POSITIVELY), limitations discussion (weigh POSITIVELY), ethical concerns (can flag for ethics committee).
- Tutorial slides: the review should contain a concise summary, a clear statement of strengths and weaknesses, and a comprehensive check for fundamental flaws; ground every judgment with a "because ...".

## What reviewers are told NOT to do
- Do not reject solely because the method does not exceed SOTA on an existing benchmark ("not grounds for rejection by itself"); tutorial lists "You must beat SOTA!" as "policy entrepreneurism".
- Do not reject for minor flaws that can be easily corrected.
- Do not commit common reviewer errors: arrogance/ignorance/inaccuracy, pure opinions, novelty fallacy, blank assertions, intellectual laziness, policy entrepreneurism (e.g., "You must publish your dataset!", "You must have a theorem!", "You must beat arXiv papers!").
- Do not reject solely for missing citations or comparisons to prior work published only without review (arXiv, technical reports). Failing to cite an arXiv paper or failing to beat its performance "SHOULD NOT be SOLE grounds for rejection." Do not reject solely because a similar idea already appeared on arXiv.
- Do not request substantial additional experiments for the rebuttal, or penalize for their absence ("Substantial" = what a major revision would need). Do not penalize papers for supplying extra results; they may be ignored.
- Do not require comparison on a withdrawn dataset (policy violation).
- Do not request comparisons to closed-source work (requiring re-implementation) as a basis for decision without appropriate justification; only request comparisons if the publication/code was available sufficiently ahead of the submission deadline.
- Do not reject for using private/restricted datasets (but these cannot be claimed as contributions).
- Do not penalize a Datasets/Benchmarks paper for not proposing a novel method, or any paper for lacking features not central to its Contribution Type; do not silently apply another type's criteria.
- Do not penalize absence of an IRB discussion when an existing public dataset is used; do not reject solely for lacking a negative-societal-impact discussion or a separate limitations section.
- Do not try to discover author identities; do not accept or reject based on known/suspected authorship.
- Do not use LLMs (see policy below); do not share the paper, results, code, or supplementary material with non-reviewers; do not use ideas/code from reviewed papers.
- Do not be belittling or sarcastic; avoid addressing authors as "you" (the guide prefers "the paper"); do not ask authors to cite several of your own papers (tutorial).
- Do not assume your stack should match the conference acceptance rate; there is no quota.

## Concurrent-work policy
No numeric cut-off (e.g., "N months before the deadline") is specified. Stated rules, verbatim:
- Reviewer FAQ: "asking authors to thoroughly compare their work with arXiv reports that appeared shortly before the submission deadline imposes an unreasonable burden. We also do not wish to discourage the publication of similar ideas that have been developed independently and concurrently." Guidelines: "Authors are not required to discuss and compare their work with recent arXiv reports, although they should properly acknowledge those that directly and obviously inspired them." / "Failing to cite an arXiv paper or failing to beat its performance SHOULD NOT be SOLE grounds for rejection." / "Reviewers SHOULD NOT reject a paper solely because another paper with a similar idea has already appeared on arXiv." / "It is acceptable for a reviewer to suggest that an author should acknowledge or be aware of something on arXiv."
- Submission Policies: "a technical report (departmental, arXiv, etc.) version of the submission that is put up without any form of direct peer-review is NOT considered prior art and does NOT NEED to be cited in the submission; authors may cite such material, but cannot be penalized for not citing it." A "publication" is "a written work longer than four pages (excluding references) that was submitted for review by peers for either acceptance or rejection, and, after review, was accepted"; arXiv preprints and technical reports are not publications. Reviewer FAQ: a prior peer-reviewed paper of four pages or less (e.g., ICIP/ICASSP) is not a dual-submission violation, but the reviewer may still judge added value.
- Closed-source comparisons (Reviewer FAQ): "comparisons should only be requested if the publication and / or code has been available sufficiently ahead of the submission deadline." ("sufficiently ahead" is not quantified.)
- Submission deadline for reference: Mar 5, 2026, 11:00 PM CET.

## Score meanings
- Rating / preliminary recommendation: unconfirmed. No official ECCV 2026 source lists the levels or labels. (The tutorial's example reviews quote ICLR ratings such as "9: Top 15% of accepted papers, strong accept"; those are ICLR examples, not the ECCV scale.)
- Confidence: unconfirmed.
- Final recommendation: unconfirmed.
- Decision levels described in the tutorial ("Level of decisions"): AWARD CANDIDATE (major advances that will heavily impact the field), ORAL (outstanding paper: top ~10% of all accepted papers), POSTER (default for all accepted papers: "works that add bricks to the cathedral of knowledge; papers introducing useful tools; papers of interest to a subcommunity; creative ideas that are hard to judge but could be promising"), REJECT ("Unlikely to be significant, or papers with major flaws"). OpenReview config accept decision options: "Accept (Oral)", "Accept (Poster)".

## Other policies relevant to feedback
- **LLM policy for reviewers** (Reviewer Guide, Reviewer FAQ, What's New, tutorial): "ECCV2026 does not allow the use of Large Language Models or online chatbots such as ChatGPT in any part of the reviewing process." FAQ: LLMs "are NOT allowed to be used to write reviews or meta-reviews, whether it is run locally or via an API." You cannot use an LLM to generate content; the review must be based on your own judgment. You cannot share substantial content from the paper or your review with an LLM (e.g., cannot use an LLM to translate a review). You can use an LLM "to do background research or to check short phrases for clarity/grammar"; tutorial adds "The use of grammar checker software based on LLMs is allowed." Reviews are checked; violations are "highly irresponsible" and lead, at PC discretion, to desk rejection of all the reviewer's own papers; PCs may report misconduct to future conferences. Submission Policies: assigned submissions "must not be uploaded to and/or processed by large language models."
- **Prompt injection**: if suspected, flag to AC/PCs. A reviewer whose LLM-generated review was swayed by a prompt injection commits a serious violation.
- **Responsible Reviewing Policy**: highly irresponsible = unreasonably short reviews without specific technical content; generic comments applicable to any paper; demonstrable factual errors indicating superficial reading; LLM-generated or LLM-assisted reviews. Not penalized: differing opinions, missing minor details, concise but substantive reviews, legitimate disagreement. Penalty: desk rejection of the reviewer's own papers (PC discretion). Late reviews: same penalty.
- **Rebuttal**: one-page PDF (two-column rebuttal template), no external links (except to existing external content such as software used). Reviewers may request clarifications or illustrations, not substantial new experiments. Authors may send a confidential comment to the AC.
- **Supplementary material**: optional to consult; paper must stand on its own; no appendices in the main paper; supplementary may not include results from an improved method or a corrected PDF (report violations to the AC). Code is encouraged, not required; reviewers may optionally check it; delete after review.
- **Page limit**: 14 pages LNCS (excluding references); wrong template/font/margins leads to desk rejection.
- **Dataset release**: Datasets/Benchmarks papers must make the dataset/benchmark public by camera-ready; non-public parts are not contributions.
- **Anonymity**: arXiv posting is not a violation; social media posts are not a violation unless they explicitly identify the paper as an ECCV submission. External links in submissions that expand content are not allowed.
- **Limitations / societal impact**: encouraged, weighed positively, not required.
- **Ethics**: flag significant concerns for the ethics committee; accepted papers must follow the Springer Nature Code of Conduct for Book Authors.
- **Confidentiality**: do not show the paper to anyone except someone asked to help with the review; destroy papers, supplementary material, and code after review.
- **Tone**: third person ("the paper"); courteous; tutorial suggests 2–4 hours per paper (at least 4 for newer reviewers).
- **Reproducibility checklist**: none mentioned for ECCV 2026 (not specified).
